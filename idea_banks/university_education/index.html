University for everyone, weighed up

Higher education opens doors to knowledge, careers and personal growth — but not every path suits every person, and pushing all young people into university can create debt, pressure and skill shortages elsewhere. Both sides below.

Four reasons everyone should go Four reasons not everyone should

+ Arguments for

💼 Better careers and income

  • Graduates often access professional and higher-paid jobs
  • Many employers still require a degree for promotion
  • Long-term earning potential is usually stronger

🧠 Knowledge and thinking skills

  • University teaches analysis, research and problem-solving
  • Students gain specialist knowledge in their field
  • These skills support lifelong learning and adaptability

🤝 Networking and social growth

  • Students meet peers, mentors and future colleagues
  • Campus life builds communication and teamwork
  • Connections can help with jobs and entrepreneurship

🌍 Broader opportunities

  • Opens paths to medicine, law, engineering and research
  • Encourages independence and confidence in young adults
  • Can reduce inequality when access is fair

− Arguments against

🔧 Vocational paths matter

  • Trades and technical jobs are essential to the economy
  • Electricians, nurses and technicians are often in high demand
  • Not all success requires a university degree

💸 Cost and student debt

  • Tuition, living costs and fees can last for years
  • Graduates may struggle to repay loans in low-paid work
  • Financial pressure can delay buying a home or starting a family

📉 Wrong fit and dropouts

  • Some students are pushed into degrees they do not want
  • Academic study does not suit every personality or talent
  • Dropouts waste time, money and confidence

⚙️ Workforce gaps

  • Society still needs skilled workers outside universities
  • Too many graduates can mean unemployment in some fields
  • Apprenticeships may prepare people better for certain jobs

A balanced system supports both university and vocational routes, so young people choose the path that matches their strengths rather than one single destination for all.

Sample essay — PTE format (discuss both views)

In a perfect world, every young person should go to university. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Higher education is often seen as the best route to a successful life, and many people believe that every young person should attend university. Others argue that this view is unrealistic, since individuals have different talents and society needs many kinds of skills. This essay will discuss both sides of the argument.

On the one hand, there are strong reasons for encouraging more young people to enter university. First, a degree can improve career opportunities and long-term income. For example, graduates in fields such as engineering, medicine and finance often access jobs that would be difficult to obtain without higher qualifications. Second, university develops critical thinking, research skills and specialist knowledge, which are valuable in a modern knowledge-based economy. Third, campus life helps students build social networks and communication skills. Meeting classmates, lecturers and industry partners can open doors to internships and future employment. Finally, wider access to university can reduce inequality when students from disadvantaged backgrounds receive support to succeed academically.

On the other hand, it does not mean that every young person should go to university. One important reason is that many essential careers do not require a degree. For instance, electricians, plumbers, mechanics and skilled healthcare workers play a vital role in society and are often well paid. If all students were pushed into academic courses, these sectors could face serious shortages. Another concern is cost. Tuition fees, accommodation and living expenses can create large student debts, and some graduates struggle to repay loans if they cannot find suitable work. In addition, not every eighteen-year-old is ready for academic study or interested in the same subjects as their parents expect. Some students drop out after one or two years, which wastes money and damages confidence. Therefore, forcing everyone into university may create pressure rather than opportunity.

In conclusion, university education offers major advantages such as better career prospects, stronger thinking skills and valuable social connections. However, vocational training, financial costs and individual differences show that higher education should not be mandatory for everyone. A better approach is to offer young people a real choice between university and practical career paths.
