Social media, weighed up

Platforms connect billions of people and spread information instantly — but they also fuel addiction, misinformation and mental health problems. Both sides below for a benefits-vs-drawbacks essay.

Four benefits Four drawbacks

+ Benefits

🌐 Connection

  • Stay in touch with friends and family across distances
  • Build communities around shared interests
  • Support networks during illness or isolation

📢 Information & awareness

  • News and public health alerts spread quickly
  • Social movements gain visibility
  • Free access to educational content and tutorials

💼 Business & careers

  • Small businesses reach customers cheaply
  • Freelancers and creators earn income online
  • Professional networking (LinkedIn, industry groups)

🎨 Expression & creativity

  • Anyone can share art, music and opinions
  • Minority voices find audiences
  • Entertainment and learning on demand

− Drawbacks

🧠 Mental health

  • Comparison with others lowers self-esteem
  • Cyberbullying and online harassment
  • Addiction and reduced sleep from scrolling

📰 Misinformation

  • False news spreads faster than fact-checking
  • Conspiracy theories and polarisation
  • Hard to know which sources are trustworthy

🔒 Privacy & security

  • Personal data collected and sold to advertisers
  • Identity theft and phishing scams
  • Location and behaviour tracked without clear consent

⏱ Productivity & real life

  • Hours wasted instead of studying or working
  • Less face-to-face conversation
  • Short attention spans from constant notifications

A balanced answer often says benefits outweigh drawbacks only with regulation, digital literacy and personal limits — not that social media is harmless.

Sample essay — PTE format (outweigh question)

Do the benefits of social media outweigh the drawbacks?

Social media has become part of daily life for billions of people worldwide. While critics point to mental health risks, misinformation and wasted time, supporters highlight connection, access to information and economic opportunity. This essay will examine both sides and argue whether the benefits outweigh the drawbacks.

On the one hand, social media offers several important benefits. First, it helps people stay connected regardless of distance. For example, migrants can video-call family abroad, and elderly people in lockdown can maintain friendships online when visits are impossible. Second, it spreads information and raises awareness quickly. During natural disasters or health emergencies, official updates reach the public within minutes. Social movements can also draw attention to injustice that traditional media might ignore. Third, social media supports business and careers. A small bakery can advertise locally on Instagram at low cost, and freelancers can find clients through LinkedIn or professional groups. Finally, it gives ordinary users a voice to share creativity, opinions and experiences that would otherwise have no platform.

On the other hand, the drawbacks are serious and growing. One major concern is mental health. Many teenagers compare themselves to edited photos online and feel inadequate, which can lead to anxiety and depression. Cyberbullying is another problem, as hurtful messages can reach victims at any hour. Another issue is misinformation. False claims about politics, medicine or elections can go viral before experts respond, damaging public trust and even putting lives at risk. In addition, privacy is weak on many platforms, because companies collect personal data for advertising and users may not understand what they are sharing. Finally, excessive use reduces productivity and replaces real-world conversation, as students and workers lose hours to endless scrolling instead of studying or resting.

In conclusion, social media clearly has both valuable benefits and real harms. However, in my opinion, the benefits do outweigh the drawbacks when platforms are used responsibly. Connection, access to information and economic opportunity are too important to reject entirely. Governments should regulate harmful content and data use, schools should teach digital literacy, and individuals should set limits — so society can enjoy the advantages while reducing the worst effects.
