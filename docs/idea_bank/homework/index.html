Homework, weighed up

Take-home tasks can strengthen learning and build discipline — but they can also increase stress, steal sleep and widen the gap between students who get help at home and those who do not. Both sides below.

Four reasons homework helps Four reasons homework harms

+ Arguments for

📖 Reinforces learning

  • Students review what they studied in class
  • Practice turns new knowledge into long-term memory
  • Teachers can check understanding before moving on

⏰ Builds discipline

  • Regular tasks teach time management and responsibility
  • Students learn to work independently without a teacher
  • Good preparation for university and working life

📝 Exam preparation

  • Extra exercises improve performance in tests
  • Writing and problem-solving skills need repetition
  • Helps weaker students catch up at their own pace

👨‍👩‍👧 Parent involvement

  • Parents can see what children are learning
  • Family support can motivate younger learners
  • Creates a link between school and home

− Arguments against

😰 Stress and burnout

  • Heavy workloads can overwhelm students
  • Pressure rises before exams and deadlines
  • Linked to anxiety, especially in high school

🌙 Less balance in life

  • Less time for sleep, sport, hobbies and friends
  • Evenings and weekends become more school-like
  • Children need rest and play for healthy development

🏠 Unequal home support

  • Not all parents can help with difficult subjects
  • Some students lack quiet space or internet at home
  • Homework can favour students from richer families

📋 Low-quality tasks

  • Some assignments are repetitive busy work
  • Copying answers does not mean real learning
  • Too much quantity may reduce motivation to study

Effective homework is purposeful, age-appropriate and limited in quantity — not simply more work for its own sake.

Sample essay — PTE format (discuss both views)

Some people believe that homework is essential for students' learning. Others think it puts too much pressure on young people. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Homework has long been a central part of school life, but it remains controversial. While some people argue that take-home tasks are essential for learning and discipline, others believe they create unnecessary stress and unfair disadvantages. This essay will discuss both views.

On the one hand, there are good reasons to support homework. First, it helps students review and practise what they learn in class. For example, a maths student who solves extra problems at home is more likely to remember formulas before a test. Without practice, new knowledge is easily forgotten. Second, homework teaches responsibility and time management. When students plan their evenings and finish tasks by a deadline, they develop habits that are useful at university and in future jobs. Third, regular assignments can improve exam results, especially in subjects such as writing and science, where skills improve through repetition. Finally, homework allows parents to stay involved in their children's education. A primary-school parent who reads with a child at home can support literacy in a way that complements classroom teaching.

On the other hand, homework also has serious drawbacks. One concern is the pressure it places on students. Many teenagers already spend long hours at school, and adding several hours of homework each night can lead to stress, tiredness and burnout. For example, students preparing for major exams may sleep too little because they are trying to finish multiple assignments. Another problem is that homework reduces time for rest and other activities. Sport, hobbies and time with friends are important for mental health and personal development, yet heavy workloads can make school life feel endless. In addition, not all students receive equal support at home. A child in a crowded house without a desk, or whose parents cannot help with English or maths, may struggle while classmates with tutors progress faster. This can widen educational inequality. Finally, some homework is poorly designed and becomes repetitive busy work that does not deepen understanding.

In conclusion, homework can support learning through practice, discipline and exam preparation, and it can involve parents in education. However, it also causes stress, imbalance, inequality and low-quality tasks when it is excessive or unfair. Therefore, homework is useful, but schools should assign less, better-targeted work rather than large amounts every day.
